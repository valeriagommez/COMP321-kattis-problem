\problemname{Spider-Man's Top Secret Operation}

% Example use of the \illustration command
\illustration{0.6}{spiderman.jpeg}{\href{https://www.cbr.com/preview-amazing-spider-man-1-fcbd/}{Amazing Spider-Man FCBD Story Art by Ryan Ottley}}

Spider-Man is the hero of New York City, flying from crime scene to crime scene to catch bad guys and keep the city safe. NYC is not exactly known as a peaceful city; having become a trusted partner of the NYPD to help them keep up with the high crime rate, he has been helping uncover a large-scale crime ring.\newline
Every day, the NYPD sends Spider-Man a list of locations from which he must extract sensitive information for the day. Every location provided has critical information to be retrieved, thus every location must be visited. Due to the highly secretive nature of the operation and to prevent being busted by the bad guys, he must make use of secret passageways available that take him from a given location to another given location.\newline
Spider-Man has become a local legend for city residents. Word travels fast in NYC, and every time he is spotted, people flock to the area within minutes, hoping to take a photo with him. He must never go back to a spot he has already inspected because citizens always arrive on site right after he leaves a location. He must avoid citizens at all cost because getting his whereabouts leaked would compromise the operation, and he could lose his job!\newline
Knowing how important this job is for our beloved hero, Doctor Strange gave him the ability to teleport from one place to another once per day. If exactly one more passage would be needed, he could make use of his teleportation ability and complete the operation.
In which order should Spider-Man visit all given locations without jeopardizing his top secret mission? 

\section*{Input}

\begin{itemize}
    \item There is a single test case in the input. Each test case will begin with a line with two integers $n$ and $m$ ($1 \leqslant n \leqslant 10^4$, $0 \leqslant m \leqslant 10^4$), the number of locations and the number of one-way passageways.
    \item Each of the next $m$ lines contains two distinct integers $x$ and $y$, meaning there is a passageway from $x$ to $y$.
\end{itemize}

\section*{Output}

If there is an ordering of all $n$ locations such that consecutive locations are connected by a passageway and Spider-Man doesn’t cross paths with citizens, print the $n$ locations in that order on one line. This ordering is unique.\newline
Otherwise, if exactly one teleport (from the current location to any unvisited location, usable between any two consecutive locations in the order) makes a valid ordering possible, print “POSSIBLE”, then on a second line print $x$ and $y$, the location Spider-Man teleports from and the location he teleports to. If several pairs work, print any of them. \newline
Otherwise, output “IMPOSSIBLE”.
